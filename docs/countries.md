# Countries for reciprocating gas-engine power plants

## Investment objective
Develop greenfield 5–10 MW natural-gas reciprocating-engine sites, targeting capacity payments, merchant electricity sales, balancing services or contracted industrial offtake.

Key screening questions for every country:
- Can an independent developer build and connect a 5–10 MW plant?
- Is there a capacity market, or another form of dependable contracted revenue?
- How can electricity be sold, and how are prices determined?
- Can the site secure gas at a price that allows profitable generation?
- What are the grid-connection, licensing and permitting requirements?

## Poland 

### Benefits:
* Established capacity-market mechanism, offering potential revenue for eligible dependable capacity.
* Established wholesale electricity market with day-ahead, intraday and balancing arrangements.
* Diversified gas-supply infrastructure, including Baltic Pipe and LNG imports.
* Electricity system transitioning away from coal, with growing renewable generation creating a need for flexibility.

### Challenges:
* Winning a capacity agreement at a commercially viable price; eligibility and bidding restrictions need to be verified.
* Grid-connection costs, available capacity and development timelines.
* Competition from other generators, storage and demand response.

## Indonesia

### Benefits:
* Potential demand for flexible and distributed generation in selected industrial areas and isolated electricity systems
* Industrial captive power or contracted supply may provide an alternative to selling into the public grid
* Opportunities to investigate diesel displacement on selected islands, provided reliable gas logistics are feasible

### Challenges:
* Electricity procurement and planning are heavily shaped by PLN
* A developer needs a viable procurement route, such as a PLN power-purchase agreement or a legally permissible industrial supply arrangement
* Gas availability, local pricing, pipeline access and transport logistics vary substantially by site.
* Licensing, investment approvals, local ownership and contractual requirements need specialist Indonesian advice
* Island-grid opportunities may be too small or remote to justify gas infrastructure

## Phillipines

### Benefits:
* Wholesale electricity spot market provides a route for eligible generators to sell electricity.
* Diesel replacement may be attractive in particular locations, but only if gas can be delivered economically and reliably.
* Opportunities to investigate industrial supply, ancillary services and selected island-grid applications.

### Challenges:
* Natural-gas supply is a major constraint for small projects outside established gas infrastructure.
* The Philippines is an archipelago: grid conditions, electricity prices and generation requirements differ between Luzon, Visayas and Mindanao.
* Spot-market revenue is volatile
* Market participation, bilateral contracts and ancillary-service qualification involve separate regulatory and technical requirements.

### To Do:
- [ ] 1. Understand the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM), including price formation, dispatch, settlement and participant registration.
- [ ] 2. Determine whether a proposed plant can participate directly or would need to contract through a trader, supplier or aggregator.
- [ ] 3. Investigate ancillary-services procurement and whether a gas engine can qualify for relevant reserve products.
- [ ] 4. Investigate the status of gas infrastructure, including the supply and transport arrangements available to a small independent generator.

## Australia

### Benefits:
* Established wholesale electricity markets with transparent dispatch and pricing.
* Potential merchant-peaking opportunities in regions experiencing scarcity, high price volatility or local network constraints.
* Gas infrastructure and an established energy-trading industry in several regions.
* In Western Australia, the Wholesale Electricity Market (WEM) includes a Reserve Capacity Mechanism, providing a separate capacity-revenue framework for eligible facilities.


### Challenges
* Australia is not one electricity market. The eastern and south-eastern National Electricity Market (NEM) differs materially from Western Australia's WEM.
* The NEM is principally an energy-only wholesale market, with no equivalent general capacity auction.
