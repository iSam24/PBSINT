International Market Review For Gas Power Generation

A desk-based screen of twelve markets across Europe, the Americas, Asia-Pacific and the Middle East and Africa, ranked on ownership freedom, transition phase, revenue support, trading access and currency. October 2026.

The short answer

Markets that pay for flexibility through capacity contracts and deep balancing products, and that let a UK company own 100% of a site, score highest. High headline returns in weaker-currency markets rank lower once risk is priced in.

Wave 1: enter now

Poland, USA, Ireland. Poland offers long capacity contracts for new low-carbon gas, the US has record capacity prices and data-centre demand, and Ireland mirrors the UK model.

Wave 2: build the case

Germany, Italy, Australia. Strong balancing and trading markets. Germany is waiting on its capacity mechanism and Australia's best support sits in Western Australia.

Watchlist or hold

Japan, South Africa, Brazil, Philippines, Vietnam, Nigeria. Large demand, but weaker currencies, thinner support or a local partner requirement.

How to proceed

Entry structure

Nine of the twelve markets allow wholly owned subsidiaries. A local partner is advisable in South Africa, Vietnam and Nigeria, and useful in Brazil, for licences, gas supply and offtake rather than legal necessity.

Next steps

  • Commission legal and tax checks on the Wave 1 markets.
  • Model site-level returns using current capacity prices and gas costs.
  • Confirm grid connection timelines, the most common delay in every market.
  • Agree a currency hedging policy before committing capital.

Screened out early

Mexico (state-led policy), Turkey (currency), India and Egypt (coal dominance, gas affordability and currency) and the Gulf states (state-procured, utility-scale projects).
